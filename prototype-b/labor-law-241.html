Construction, excavation and demolition injuries may involve safety duties under Labor Law § 241(6), often tied to a specific New York Industrial Code provision. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

How § 241(6) Works

Labor Law § 241(6) imposes safety-related duties on certain owners, contractors and agents during covered work. A claim generally must identify an applicable and sufficiently specific Industrial Code regulation that was violated.

A general allegation that a site was unsafe is not enough by itself. The exact regulation and facts matter.

Examples of Safety Issues

Potential issues include tripping hazards in passageways, unsafe work areas, inadequate protection around certain openings, or other conditions addressed by a specific regulation. Not every hazard violates a qualifying rule.

Attorneys evaluate the Industrial Code, the worker’s activity, and whether the violation caused the injury.

How It Differs From § 240

Section 240 focuses on particular elevation-related hazards and has distinct liability rules. Section 241(6) involves qualifying construction safety regulations and may permit comparative-negligence arguments. Section 200 involves a separate duty and different proof.

Preserving Jobsite Evidence

  • Record the exact location and condition if it is safe to do so
  • Identify witnesses and contractors
  • Preserve photographs, incident reports and medical records
  • Seek advice promptly about potential claims

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know the Industrial Code rule before calling?

No. You can describe what happened; counsel can determine which provisions may apply.

Does § 241(6) cover every construction accident?

No. The work, specific safety regulation, violation and causation must be established.
