New York provides special civil filing rules for certain people who experienced sexual abuse as children. These rules can differ substantially from ordinary personal injury deadlines, and the date and nature of the alleged conduct matter.

CPLR § 208(b): Qualifying Childhood Sexual Abuse

For qualifying childhood sexual-abuse conduct, CPLR § 208(b) generally permits certain civil claims to be commenced on or before the survivor reaches age 55. The statute identifies the conduct covered and addresses claims against parties whose intentional or negligent acts or omissions are alleged to have resulted in that conduct.

Not every form of mistreatment or physical abuse qualifies under this special provision. An attorney should analyze the alleged conduct, defendants, and applicable exceptions.

The Child Victims Act Revival Window Has Closed

The Child Victims Act also created a temporary revival window for certain otherwise time-barred childhood sexual-abuse claims under CPLR § 214-g. That special window is closed. The fact that the revival window ended does not mean every other potential claim is necessarily time-barred.

Public Schools and Notice Requirements

Claims against public entities may involve separate notice-of-claim procedures under General Municipal Law § 50-e. Some special sexual-abuse provisions override certain notice requirements for covered claims. Do not assume that the same rules apply to all claims or defendants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an adult sue for abuse that happened in childhood?

Sometimes. CPLR § 208(b) can apply to qualifying childhood sexual-abuse conduct. Timeliness depends on the facts and other applicable law.

Does every claim have until age 55?

No. The statute has specific coverage requirements, and other claims may have different deadlines.

Does a criminal conviction have to occur first?

A civil claim may be evaluated independently of a criminal prosecution, subject to the particular statute and legal theory.

What if the school is public?

Public entities may have additional procedural requirements, with important statutory exceptions for certain qualifying claims.
