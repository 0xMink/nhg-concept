When a pedestrian is struck by a car, truck or other vehicle, the legal analysis often focuses on driver due care, crosswalk rules, traffic signals, visibility and causation. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

Drivers Have a Duty of Care

Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1146 requires drivers to exercise due care to avoid collisions with pedestrians. The duty is not limited to marked crosswalks. Whether a driver met that duty depends on the circumstances.

Crosswalks and Traffic Signals

VTL § 1151 addresses right of way at crosswalks when traffic-control signals are not operating or present, while other rules govern signal-controlled crossings. A pedestrian’s actions and applicable signals can also be relevant.

A crosswalk does not make every collision automatically the driver’s fault; the evidence must be evaluated.

Evidence That May Establish Fault

  • Intersection and vehicle-camera footage
  • Traffic signal timing and crosswalk configuration
  • Witness statements and police reports
  • Photographs, lighting, weather and sight lines
  • Medical records documenting the nature and timing of injuries

Insurance, Injuries and Deadlines

A pedestrian struck by a motor vehicle may have rights under New York no-fault law as well as a potential liability claim. The serious-injury threshold and the 2026 motor-vehicle amendments may be relevant in certain actions.

Claims against municipalities can have additional notice requirements, so early review is important.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bring a claim if I was not in a crosswalk?

Possibly. Drivers still owe a duty of due care, but pedestrian conduct and comparative fault may affect the case.

Does a traffic ticket determine civil liability?

Not by itself. The civil case depends on admissible evidence and applicable legal rules.

What if a city-owned vehicle struck me?

Special public-entity notice and filing rules may apply, and timing can be critical.
