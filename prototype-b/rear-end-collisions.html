Is the Rear Driver Always at Fault?

No. Under New York case law, a rear-end collision with a stopped or stopping vehicle commonly creates a prima facie inference of negligence by the rear driver, but that inference may be rebutted by an adequate nonnegligent explanation.

The surrounding facts—traffic, road conditions, sudden events and vehicle behavior—must be examined.

The Following-Distance Rule

Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1129 requires a driver to avoid following another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, considering speed, traffic and road conditions. A violation may be relevant evidence of negligence.

Owner Liability and Injury Claims

Vehicle ownership may matter under § 388. Recovering pain and suffering may also depend on New York’s motor-vehicle insurance and serious-injury rules.

Useful Evidence

  • Dashcam or nearby surveillance footage
  • Damage photographs and repair records
  • Witness contact information
  • Medical records and contemporaneous symptoms

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the car in front stopped suddenly?

A sudden stop may be relevant, but it does not automatically excuse the following driver. The facts and applicable case law matter.

What if I had a preexisting injury?

Medical causation and aggravation can be evaluated using prior and current records.
