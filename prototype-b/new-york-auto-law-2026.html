New York Auto Accident Law Changes in 2026

Which Cases Do the Changes Affect?

Part EE of Chapter 58 of the Laws of 2026 took effect May 26, 2026 and applies to actions and proceedings commenced on or after that date. The filing date can matter even if the accident occurred earlier.

This is a significant change for injured people considering a lawsuit, and it warrants a case-specific review.

The Serious-Injury Definition Changed

The 2026 legislation removed the former category involving a medically determined nonpermanent injury that prevented substantially all usual activities for at least 90 of the first 180 days. Other statutory serious-injury categories remain, including fractures, significant disfigurement and certain permanent or significant limitations.

Whether medical evidence meets an available category is a separate question from whether the other driver was negligent.

Comparative Fault Changed for Covered Claims

New CPLR § 1411(b) generally bars recovery in covered motor-vehicle personal injury actions if the claimant’s culpable conduct is greater than the fault of the defendant or the defendants combined. This differs from the prior pure-comparative-negligence approach.

The amended rule is limited to actions subject to Insurance Law Article 51 and should not be described as applying to every New York negligence claim.

Additional Rules and Damage Limits

The amendments also changed the sequencing of fault and serious-injury determinations and established a $100,000 limit on noneconomic damages in narrowly defined circumstances involving certain at-fault drivers, with statutory exceptions.

The Department of Financial Services has published guidance explaining these reforms. The exact statutory language should be reviewed before any conclusion about a particular case.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did New York eliminate the 90/180-day serious-injury category?

Yes, for actions and proceedings governed by the amendments effective May 26, 2026. Other serious-injury categories remain.

Can someone more than 50% at fault recover in a covered auto injury case?

The amended CPLR § 1411(b) generally bars recovery when the claimant’s fault is greater than the defendant or defendants combined, in actions subject to Article 51.

Does the new law apply to a lawsuit filed before May 26, 2026?

The statutory amendments described here apply to actions and proceedings commenced on or after May 26, 2026. The specific procedural history should be reviewed.
