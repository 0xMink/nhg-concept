A medical malpractice case involves more than an unfavorable outcome. The legal analysis considers the applicable professional standard of care, causation and the injury. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

What Must Generally Be Proven?

Medical malpractice claims commonly require proof of a departure from accepted standards of medical care and that the departure caused a legally compensable injury. Expert medical analysis is often necessary.

A known complication or unsuccessful treatment does not automatically establish malpractice.

What Evidence Matters?

Complete medical records, imaging, treatment timelines, informed-consent documentation and expert review can be central to evaluating a claim.

New York Filing Deadlines

CPLR § 214-a generally provides two years and six months for medical malpractice actions, with significant exceptions, including certain continuous-treatment, foreign-object and cancer-diagnosis situations. Public-entity claims may raise additional rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every surgical complication malpractice?

No. A departure from the applicable standard of care and causation generally must be established.

Is the deadline always two years and six months?

No. Several statutory exceptions and special defendant rules may apply.
