What Determines Liability?

Different accidents involve different legal rules. Construction claims may implicate Labor Law §§ 240 and 241; automobile claims may involve the Vehicle and Traffic Law and New York's no-fault framework; premises cases often turn on a dangerous condition and actual or constructive notice.

Our legal guides explain the distinctions without suggesting that every accident creates a lawsuit.

Institutional cases may involve Public Health Law § 2801-d for qualifying nursing homes or separate duties for assisted living and adult care facilities. Commercial truck and pedestrian accidents may raise additional vehicle and traffic safety questions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of injury cases does NHG handle?

The practice includes vehicle and pedestrian accidents, construction injuries, slip-and-falls, institutional negligence and other personal injury matters. Explore the guides above.

What should I do after an accident?

Seek appropriate medical attention. If it is safe, preserve relevant photographs, records and witness information. An attorney can help assess your circumstances.

How long do I have to bring a claim?

Deadlines depend on the type of claim and the parties involved. Some notice requirements may apply much sooner than a lawsuit deadline. Speak with an attorney promptly.
