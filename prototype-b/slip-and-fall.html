A property owner or other responsible party may face liability when a dangerous condition causes an injury and the elements of negligence are established. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

When Is a Property Owner Responsible?

Many New York premises-liability claims require proof that a responsible party created the dangerous condition or had actual or constructive notice of it and failed to act reasonably. A fall alone does not prove negligence.

Who controlled the property, the condition involved, and how long the hazard existed can be central questions.

Actual and Constructive Notice

Actual notice concerns a known hazard. Constructive notice generally concerns a condition that was visible and apparent and existed long enough that it reasonably should have been discovered and corrected.

Surveillance footage, cleaning logs, inspection routines and witness accounts can help establish or rebut notice.

Common Locations

Claims can arise in stores, apartment buildings, parking areas, stairwells, sidewalks and other properties. Snow and ice claims may involve additional doctrines and municipal rules.

Deadlines and Public Property

A personal injury action often has a three-year limitation period, but claims against certain municipalities or public entities can require a notice of claim much sooner. Never assume a general deadline applies to every location.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a store owe compensation whenever someone slips?

No. Negligence, notice, causation and damages must be established.

What if the fall happened on a sidewalk?

Responsibility varies by municipality and property type. See the separate sidewalk guide.
