Does an Unfair Firing Mean an Illegal Firing?

Not necessarily. In many jobs, an employer can terminate employment for reasons that feel unfair without violating the law. Claims may arise when the firing involves unlawful discrimination, retaliation, breach of an enforceable agreement, or another recognized legal protection.

Examples of Potentially Unlawful Reasons

Terminations related to protected characteristics, certain wage complaints, legally protected leave or other protected conduct may warrant investigation. The specific facts and statute are important.

Evidence and Timing

Offer letters, handbooks, performance reviews, emails, disciplinary records, complaint history and termination notices may be relevant. Filing deadlines differ among statutes and forums.

Compensation and Other Remedies

Depending on the claim, potential remedies may include lost pay, other damages or equitable relief. Not every claim permits the same remedies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sue because my boss was unfair?

Unfair treatment alone may not be unlawful. The reason for termination and any applicable legal protection matter.

Can I have a claim if I was fired after reporting harassment?

Possibly. Anti-retaliation laws may apply depending on what was reported and the employer’s response.
