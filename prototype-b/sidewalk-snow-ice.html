Who Must Maintain a Sidewalk?

Responsibility can differ between New York City and Long Island municipalities. In New York City, Administrative Code § 7-210 generally shifts tort liability for certain sidewalk defects to adjoining property owners, with specified exceptions.

Outside NYC, local ordinances, municipal prior-written-notice laws, ownership and control may lead to different results.

Snow, Ice and the Storm-in-Progress Rule

In many New York cases, a property owner is not required to clear naturally accumulating snow and ice while a storm is ongoing and for a reasonable period afterward. Exceptions and different factual scenarios can affect the analysis.

Municipal Notice Rules

Certain claims against public entities require a notice of claim within 90 days under General Municipal Law § 50-e. Separate prior-written-notice requirements may also be relevant to roadway or sidewalk defects.

Evidence That Helps

  • Photographs of the location and defect
  • Weather records and timing
  • Witness statements and nearby surveillance
  • Prior complaints, repair records and maintenance logs

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the city always pay for sidewalk injuries?

No. Responsibility depends on local law, property ownership and the type of defect.

Can I sue after falling during a snowstorm?

Possibly, but the storm-in-progress doctrine and other facts may affect liability.
