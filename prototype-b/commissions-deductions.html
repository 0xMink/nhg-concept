Unpaid Commissions and Unlawful Wage Deductions

Employees may have legal remedies when earned commissions are withheld or employers deduct money from wages without legal authorization. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

When Are Commissions Wages?

Whether a commission is an earned wage can depend on the compensation agreement, when the commission became due, the worker’s classification, and applicable New York law. Disputes sometimes involve contract claims as well as statutory wage claims.

Commission Agreements and Payment Timing

Labor Law § 191 contains rules for commission salespersons, including written terms addressing how commissions are calculated and paid. The precise rules depend on the worker and arrangement.

Which Deductions Are Permitted?

Labor Law § 193 restricts deductions from wages. Certain deductions required by law or voluntarily authorized in writing for the employee’s benefit may be allowed, but employers cannot simply subtract amounts whenever they choose.

What Evidence Matters?

  • Signed commission plans and offer letters
  • Sales reports and commission statements
  • Pay stubs, deductions and correspondence
  • Evidence of completed transactions and termination dates

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer keep my commissions after I resign?

That depends on when they were earned and the agreement and law governing payment.

Can my employer deduct the cost of a mistake?

Many deductions are restricted. The particular deduction and circumstances require review.
