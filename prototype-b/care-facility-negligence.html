Which Type of Facility Is Involved?

An assisted living residence, adult care facility, skilled nursing home and group home may be subject to different licensing rules and duties. The facility’s actual legal classification is an essential first step.

Do not assume nursing home laws apply to every facility providing housing or care.

Possible Bases for Liability

A claim may involve negligent supervision, unsafe premises, failure to follow an individualized service plan, breach of a legal or contractual duty, or negligent hiring or retention. Whether the facility had notice of a hazard can matter.

Rights of Residents

Public Health Law § 4660 addresses rights in covered assisted living residences, while Social Services Law § 461-d concerns adult care facilities. These provisions help identify relevant rights, but whether they support a particular civil remedy requires legal analysis.

Documents to Review

  • Residency agreement and service plan
  • Facility license or certification category
  • Incident and medication reports
  • Complaints, inspections and communications with staff
  • Medical records, photographs and witness information

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Public Health Law § 2801-d apply to assisted living?

Not automatically. That statute applies to qualifying residential health care facilities, and assisted living is generally a different regulatory category.

What if a resident fell because no one helped them?

Liability may depend on the resident’s needs, the facility’s promised services, known risks, staffing, and causation.

Can a resident or family member obtain records?

Access depends on authorization, privacy rules and the type of records. Counsel can help identify appropriate requests.
