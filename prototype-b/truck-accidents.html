Who May Be Responsible?

Potential defendants may include a negligent driver, a company legally responsible for the driver’s conduct, a vehicle owner, or another party whose acts contributed to the collision. Liability is not automatic simply because a commercial truck was involved.

Ownership, permission to operate, employment relationships, contracts and federal limitations can all affect who may be sued.

How Truck Crashes Differ from Car Accidents

Large commercial vehicles can have longer stopping distances and significant blind spots. Relevant facts may include speed, following distance, fatigue, loading, inspections, maintenance and driver training.

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations may apply to some commercial operations, but coverage must be established; they do not automatically govern every delivery vehicle.

Evidence to Preserve

  • Police and incident reports; witness names and video
  • Driver logs and electronic logging device information where applicable
  • Dispatch records, vehicle inspection and maintenance records
  • Company and insurance information; photographs of the vehicles and roadway
  • Medical records and lost-earnings documentation

Compensation and Time Limits

New York no-fault and serious-injury rules may affect certain truck-collision claims, and the 2026 amendments can matter depending on the action’s commencement date. Other types of claims may be governed by different rules.

Potential damages depend on fault, causation, injuries and applicable law. Prompt preservation requests may be important because electronic records may not be retained indefinitely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the trucking company be liable for a driver’s mistake?

Sometimes. Employment, agency, ownership and other legal theories may support liability, depending on the facts.

Do federal trucking regulations apply to every delivery truck?

No. Federal rules have scope and coverage requirements; some operations are governed by other rules.

Is a truck accident case the same as a car accident case?

They share many negligence and insurance issues, but commercial vehicles can raise additional evidence and regulatory questions.
