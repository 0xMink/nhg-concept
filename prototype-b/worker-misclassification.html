A 1099 form or independent-contractor label does not by itself determine whether a worker is legally entitled to wage-and-hour protections. The information below explains important New York legal issues; it is not a determination that a particular claim is valid or timely.

Why Classification Matters

Employee status can affect minimum wage, overtime and other protections. The legal tests differ among federal wage law, New York wage law, unemployment and other statutes.

What Factors Are Considered?

The degree of control, nature of the work, economic relationship and other facts can matter. No single label or tax form automatically answers the question.

What if I Work Full-Time for One Company?

Working for one company can be relevant, but it is not conclusive. An attorney should examine the actual relationship rather than rely on a contract’s terminology.

Potential Wage Recovery

If a worker was improperly classified, unpaid overtime or minimum wages and other remedies may be available, depending on the applicable law and claim period.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does receiving a 1099 mean I cannot claim overtime?

No. Classification depends on legal standards and actual working conditions.

Can a worker be an employee under one law but not another?

Yes. Different statutes can use different tests.
