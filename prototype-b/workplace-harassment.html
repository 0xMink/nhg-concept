Workplace Harassment & Retaliation in New York

Employees often ask whether their experience was serious enough to qualify as unlawful harassment. New York law contains protections that differ from older federal standards, but not every unpleasant workplace interaction is illegal.

Sexual Harassment Under New York Law

Executive Law § 296(1)(h) addresses harassment based on protected characteristics, including sex. Covered harassment need not be “severe or pervasive” to be unlawful; the statute instead focuses on inferior terms, conditions, or privileges of employment and recognizes a defense involving petty slights or trivial inconveniences.

Conduct can include unwelcome sexual advances, sexual comments, coercive requests, and other behavior depending on the circumstances.

Retaliation for Reporting Misconduct

New York law prohibits certain adverse actions because a person opposed unlawful practices, filed a complaint, or participated in protected proceedings. Retaliation claims require analysis of the protected activity, the response, and the connection between them.

Evidence to Preserve When Safe and Lawful

  • Messages, emails, and written complaints.
  • Dates and details of incidents and witnesses.
  • Changes in work assignments, pay, scheduling, or employment status.
  • Relevant employer policies and responses.
Do not take confidential company documents you are not legally entitled to possess. An attorney can discuss appropriate evidence preservation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to report harassment to HR before speaking with a lawyer?

You can seek legal advice without first filing an internal report. Whether reporting is relevant to a particular claim depends on the facts and applicable law.

Can retaliation happen even if I remain employed?

Potentially. A materially harmful response may take different forms. Whether conduct is legally actionable depends on the governing law.

What if the harasser owns the company?

An owner’s involvement can be legally significant, but liability and remedies depend on the applicable claims and facts.
