What Is Institutional Negligence?

Institutional negligence involves an alleged failure by an organization to meet a legal duty owed to a person in its care. Depending on the setting, examples may include inadequate supervision, unsafe premises, failure to respond to known risks or negligent hiring and retention.

A poor outcome alone does not establish liability. The applicable duty, breach, causation and injury must be analyzed.

Different Facilities, Different Laws

Skilled nursing homes, assisted living residences, adult care facilities, group homes and other settings are regulated differently. A statute applying to one category should not be assumed to apply to another.

For example, Public Health Law § 2801-d provides a particular cause of action for patients of qualifying residential health care facilities; assisted living and adult care settings have separate statutory frameworks.

Common Injuries and Failures

  • Falls due to inadequate supervision or missing assistive devices
  • Pressure injuries, dehydration, malnutrition or medication errors
  • Unaddressed reports of abuse, neglect or unsafe conditions
  • Failures to monitor vulnerable residents or respond to emergencies

Investigating an Institutional Claim

Records may include admission assessments, care plans, incident reports, staffing records, facility policies, complaints and inspection findings. Medical and expert analysis may be necessary.

Different claims have different limitation periods, defendants and procedural requirements; institutional status must be verified early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can every care facility be sued under Public Health Law § 2801-d?

No. That provision concerns qualifying residential health care facilities; other facilities may be governed by different statutes and negligence principles.

What if the injury happened during an ordinary fall?

A fall is not automatically negligence. The care plan, known fall risk, supervision and causation may be important.

What if the facility is publicly operated?

Additional notice or filing requirements may apply to some public entities.
