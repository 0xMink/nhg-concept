What Counts as Protected Activity?

Protected activity may include making a good-faith wage complaint, opposing unlawful discrimination, participating in certain investigations, or engaging in activity covered by whistleblower statutes. Different laws protect different conduct.

What Can Retaliation Look Like?

Retaliation can involve termination, threats, demotion, reduced hours, pay changes or other adverse treatment. Whether a particular response is actionable depends on the governing law and evidence connecting it to protected conduct.

Important New York Protections

Labor Law § 215 addresses retaliation related to Labor Law rights. Executive Law § 296 protects certain opposition to discrimination and participation in proceedings. Labor Law § 740 has distinct whistleblower protections and requirements.

Evidence to Preserve

  • Copies of complaints or reports made
  • Dates and descriptions of employer responses
  • Scheduling, compensation and disciplinary records
  • Witnesses and relevant communications

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I be retaliated against without being fired?

Potentially. Other adverse treatment can qualify under the applicable statute.

Do all complaints receive the same protection?

No. Different statutes cover different subjects and have different elements and deadlines.
